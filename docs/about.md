# ► About

**BlogR Directory** is a hand-curated collection of independent blogs, human-written websites, and those little corners of the internet that are still worth getting lost in.

No endless scrolling through the same recycled articles. Just **real people, writing about things they actually care about.**

## I miss the old internet

Back in the days of **GeoCities and Angelfire**, you could click a random link and have someone's personality practically **slap you in the face**.

Huge photos that took forever to load. Questionable colour schemes. Animated GIFs everywhere. Visitor counters, guestbooks, flashing text, and music that started playing whether you wanted it to or not.

Some of those websites were absolutely hideous.

**But they were brilliant.**

You never quite knew what you were going to find. One minute you'd be reading about someone's pet hamster, the next you'd be looking at a collection of old computers, someone's holiday photos, or a website dedicated entirely to their favourite film.

> The internet used to feel like opening a random door and finding someone's entire personality on the other side.

And the best bit? People made those websites because they **wanted to**.

Not because they were chasing an algorithm, trying to rank on Google, or selling you something.

**They just had something to share.**

---

## So, what happened?

Somewhere along the way, the internet started feeling a bit less personal.

Search results became dominated by the same big websites. Social media swallowed up personal homepages. And now we've got AI-generated articles popping up everywhere, often saying an awful lot without really saying anything.

I'm not against technology moving forward. I just miss being able to stumble across a website and immediately get a sense of the person behind it.

Someone with an obsession for old horror films. Someone documenting every mile of a coastal walk. Someone who's spent twenty years writing about their garden.

**Those are the websites I want to find.**

And I reckon I'm not the only one.

---

## Why I started BlogR

I started BlogR because I wanted somewhere to collect and share those websites.

Not the biggest directory on the internet. Not a list of whoever has the best SEO. Just a place where you can browse around, click something interesting, and hopefully discover a blog you didn't know existed.

Every site is **found, reviewed and categorised by hand**.

I'm particularly interested in:

- **Personal blogs** with a bit of character.
- **Niche interests** that someone could happily talk about for hours.
- **Independent writers** sharing their experiences and opinions.
- **Useful little websites** built because someone wanted them to exist.
- **Older blogs** that are still sitting there, full of brilliant writing, even if they haven't been updated in years.

A blog doesn't need thousands of readers, a fancy design or a regular publishing schedule to belong here.

**If someone's put a bit of themselves into their website, that's what interests me.**

---

## Keeping the personal web alive

I'm not expecting the internet to go back to GeoCities.

And I'm certainly not campaigning for the return of autoplay MIDI music. Although I wouldn't complain about a few more ridiculous animated GIFs.

But I do think there's still room for an internet that feels a bit more human.

One where people write because they enjoy it. Where a tiny personal blog can be every bit as interesting as a major publication. Where following a link can take you somewhere completely unexpected.

**That's what I hope BlogR can help with.**
